<ol><li>High expectations (by myself and those around me) increase stakes.</li>
<li>I have an idea of where the line is between &ldquo;ready to ship&rdquo; and &ldquo;needs more edits&rdquo; without needing hard deadlines, but need more worldly experience.</li>
<li>Writing this article is quite different than the work I did as a research assistant at the Office of the President, with Dr. Clay or Dr. Bruhn, or for the various campaigns and social movements I worked with.</li>
<li>Stylistic expectations are kind of important, and the guidelines were not thorough enough for me to generate rational expectations.</li>
<li>After breaking what I now consider an <em>incredibly </em>unrealistic deadline in early September, questioning whether the quality of work justifies the delay is rational.</li>
</ol><p>Nothing game-breaking. I guess learning to live as an academic (or as a person) is to get comfortable with these doubts and fears, maybe figuring out where to draw the lines in some cases.</p>
<p>Adorno wrote about the strong disparities between how materially comfortable modern life is and how mentally discomfiting all the luxuries get. It appears my experience is no exception.</p>
