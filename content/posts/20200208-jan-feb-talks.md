Man, it's been a busy start to 2020. I've had such a great time talking to folks about new ideas on cities, spatial statistics, and machine learning. Back in late January, I gave a talk on our now-published paper on social boundaries in cities at [CUSP London](https://cusplondon.ac.uk/2020/01/15/cusp-london-seminar-series-28th-january-2020-dr-levi-john-wolf/) ([slides](https://ljwolf.org/talks/2020_kings_cusptalk.pdf)), invited by my gracious and wonderful host, [Jon Reades](https://twitter.com/jreades). Then, I went off to western Ireland for some short R&R, catching gale force winds at the Cliffs of Moher & some nice evenings in [Dingle, Co. Kerry.](https://www.instagram.com/p/B8TVm0CB3cb/). Then, I headed off to [Exeter to give a *different* talk about multilevel models & geography](https://www.exeter.ac.uk/news/events/details/index.php?event=10038) ([slides](https://ljwolf.org/talks/2020_exeter_spmlm.pdf)). And, finally, I'm headed off to Arizona State University to talk with my friend [Taylor Oshan](https://geospatial.umd.edu/facultyprofile/oshan/taylor) about a taxonomy of geographical "scale" in quantitative geography ([slides](http://ljwolf.org/talks/2020_asu_scale.pdf)). Oof!

Presenting your work is one of the more enjoyable parts of the job, I think, so this has been such an energizing period! At the same time, I'm looking forward to slowing down a bit later in the spring 😀.
