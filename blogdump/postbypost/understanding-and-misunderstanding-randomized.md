<p>A similar version of this comes in from King&rsquo;s &ldquo;How Robust Standard Errors Expose Methodological Problems They Do Not Fix, and What to Do About It&rdquo; from 2014&rsquo;s <em>Political Analysis</em>, and I feel matters in the same way that Geographical Analysis &amp; Spatial Statistics cannot simply be about fixing specifications to account for correlated error.</p>

<p>What&rsquo;s your theory for <em>why</em> the misspecification exists? <em>Why</em> is the error correlated? Is there a way you can directly account for that fact (e.g. <a href="https://asu.pure.elsevier.com/en/publications/a-new-set-of-spatial-interaction-models-the-theory-of-competing-d" target="_blank">competing destinations</a> from interaction modelling) instead of applying a theory-free treatment for it?</p>

<p>I&rsquo;m not sure if spatial confouding &amp; corrections are necessarily as fundamental as the principles behind RCT, but I&rsquo;m sure that, as statistics struggles to define itself alongside (or inside of) data science, spatial stats will have to carve a similar niche.</p>
